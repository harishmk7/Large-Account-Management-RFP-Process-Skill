# Large Account Management & RFP Process Skill

## Overview

This skill encodes a complete Large Account Management and RFP excellence framework drawn from
an 82-slide enterprise sales training deck. It covers four integrated workstreams:

1. **Account Prioritization** — Scoring and tiering accounts by attractiveness and ability to win
2. **Account Planning** — Sweet-spot identification, engagement models, and people/governance
3. **RFP / Bid Excellence** — Four-component bid process from qualification through defense
4. **Performance Management** — Tracking, incentives, and capability-building

Use this skill to structure account plans, qualify and score bids, build win strategies, define
bid teams, and run RFP response processes for complex B2B deals across IT services, consulting,
SaaS, and professional services.

## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **NN** | Net-New — a brand new customer your organization has never sold to |
| **EE** | Existing Expand — growing revenue within an existing account, same business unit |
| **EN** | Expand New — growing into a new business unit or division within an existing customer |
| **TCV** | Total Contract Value — the full value of a deal over its entire contract term |
| **AM** | Account Manager — owns the ongoing client relationship |
| **CP** | Client Partner — senior relationship owner, often above AM level |
| **LOB** | Line of Business — a practice area, service division, or product unit within your org |
| **BMG** | Bid Management Group — your internal team that coordinates and manages RFP responses |
| **VSG** | Vertical Solutions Group — your internal solutions or pre-sales practice by industry vertical |
| **CSAT** | Customer Satisfaction score — a periodic rating from the client on your performance |
| **SoW** | Share of Wallet — the percentage of a client's total spend that goes to your organization |
| **SPOC** | Single Point of Contact — one accountable owner for a process or relationship |
| **RAPID** | Decision framework: Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, Decide |
| **OA Scorecard** | Opportunity Assessment Scorecard — the bid/no-bid qualification tool |

## Part 1: Strategic Shift — From Hunting to Farming

The foundational shift in enterprise account management:

| Past | Present |
|------|---------|
| Net-New (NN) focus | Existing Expand (EE) and Expand New (EN) focus |
| Hunting | Farming |
| Ad hoc target setting | Account-based planning |
| Ad hoc roles | Competency-based roles |

**Target growth rate for EN accounts: 30%+**

The shift does not mean abandoning NN. It means that the majority of quota attainment comes from
growing existing relationships rather than constantly chasing new logos. NN remains important for
filling the top of the funnel — but farming disciplines (account planning, engagement models,
CSAT management) determine whether growth compounds year over year.

## Part 2: Account Management Framework

Good account management requires doing two things well:

```
Account Prioritization → Account Planning → Engagement Model → People & Capabilities → Performance Management
```

Each feeds the next. Skip prioritization and you waste planning cycles on the wrong accounts.
Skip planning and your engagement model has no foundation. Skip the engagement model and your
relationship stalls at the transactional level. All five elements must run together.

## Part 3: Account Prioritization Matrix

Use this matrix to rank accounts by where you have the biggest untapped opportunity AND the
strongest chance of winning. Score every account on two axes, then plot them in the quadrant.

### Step 1 — Attractiveness Scoring (How big is the prize?)

For each account, calculate the untapped potential (E):

| Field | Formula | Notes |
|-------|---------|-------|
| **A** | Client's total annual spend in your category ($M) | For IT services: typically 2–3% of revenue. For R&D: 8–15%. For SaaS: varies by sector. |
| **B** | Serviceable % — the portion you could realistically address | Use 80% as a starting point; adjust based on your service scope |
| **C** | Total addressable potential | C = A × B × **[multiplier]** — see note below |
| **D** | Currently captured by incumbents or in-house teams ($M) | Sourced from discovery conversations or public data |
| **E** | **Untapped Potential** | **E = C − D** — this is your headline attractiveness number |

> **Multiplier note:** The original framework used 0.5 to reflect offshore IT labor arbitrage
> (costs ~50% of onshore). Adjust this to match your own model:
> - Offshore IT/services delivery: **0.5**
> - Hybrid on/offshore: **0.65–0.75**
> - On-site or consulting: **1.0**
> - SaaS (margin-based model): **use gross margin % instead**

### Step 2 — Ability to Win Scoring (How likely are you to capture it?)

Score each account on four dimensions using a 1–7 scale. Average them to get J.

| Dimension | 1 (Weakest) | 4 (Neutral) | 7 (Strongest) |
|-----------|------------|-------------|--------------|
| **F** — Competitive positioning | New entrant; no foothold | Shared position with 1–2 competitors | Exclusive or only credible alternative |
| **G** — Internal capability | Lead position in <30% of relevant services | Lead in ~50% | Lead in >80% of relevant services |
| **H** — Strength of relationship | Greeting level only (name recognition) | Idea/Fact level (know their business) | Total level (go beyond the transaction in any setting) |
| **I** — CSAT / client satisfaction | Low or unknown | Adequate; no red flags | High; client is an active advocate |
| **J — Ability to Win composite** | | **J = (F + G + H + I) ÷ 4** | |

> **For new prospects where CSAT doesn't exist yet:** substitute "perceived switching cost" or
> "relationship warmth" — how open is the prospect to your organization specifically?

#### Full Competitive Intensity Scale (F)
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 7 | Exclusive or only credible alternative in their eyes |
| 6 | Front runner with >30% lead over nearest competitor |
| 5 | Front runner with <30% lead |
| 4 | Shared position — you and 1–2 others are equally considered |
| 3 | Not front runner; >30% gap behind the leader |
| 2 | Not front runner; <30% gap behind the leader |
| 1 | New entrant — no established presence or recognition |

#### Full Capability Scale (G)
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 7 | Lead position in >80% of services this account needs |
| 6 | Lead in >70% |
| 5 | Lead in >60% |
| 4 | Lead in >50% |
| 3 | Lead in >40% |
| 2 | Lead in >30% |
| 1 | Lead in <30% |

#### Depth of Relationship (H) — Six Levels
| Level | What you can do with this contact |
|-------|----------------------------------|
| **Total** | Go beyond the transaction; understand and relate to them in any professional or personal context |
| **Emotion** | Relate to the emotional and personal context behind their business decisions |
| **Feeling** | Understand the rational logic behind their business judgments |
| **Idea** | Know their business priorities and strategic objectives |
| **Fact** | Know the operational details of their business |
| **Greeting** | Recognize the name and role; little else |

#### Strength of Relationship Scale (H) — applied across all stakeholders
| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 7 | All key stakeholders at Total level |
| 6 | Majority at Total level |
| 5 | Majority at Emotion level |
| 4 | Majority at Feeling level |
| 3 | Majority at Idea level |
| 2 | Majority at Fact level |
| 1 | Majority at Greeting level only |

### Step 3 — Plot the Prioritization Matrix

Plot each account with **E (Untapped Potential, $M)** on the X-axis and **J (Ability to Win,
1–7)** on the Y-axis. Bubble size = incremental opportunity in $M.

```
High
Ability    ┌────────────────────┬────────────────────┐
to Win     │                    │                    │
  (J)      │  DEEPEN            │  PURSUE            │
           │  CAPABILITY        │  AGGRESSIVELY      │
    4  ────┼────────────────────┼────────────────────┤
           │                    │                    │
           │  PURSUE            │  MAINTAIN          │
           │  OPPORTUNISTICALLY │  SHARE             │
Low        └────────────────────┴────────────────────┘
                Low E                    High E
           (Low Untapped $)         (High Untapped $)
```

| Quadrant | Strategy |
|----------|---------|
| **Top-right** (High E, High J) | Pursue Aggressively — maximum investment, exec sponsorship, full account plan |
| **Top-left** (Low E, High J) | Maintain Share — protect and defend; don't over-invest |
| **Bottom-right** (High E, Low J) | Deepen Capability — build the relationship and credentials before pursuing |
| **Bottom-left** (Low E, Low J) | Pursue Opportunistically — respond if inbound; don't initiate |

### Worked Example — Scoring Two Accounts

| | Account A | Account B |
|--|-----------|-----------|
| Annual category spend (A) | $20M | $8M |
| Serviceable % (B) | 80% | 80% |
| Multiplier | 0.7 (hybrid) | 0.7 |
| Total potential (C) | $11.2M | $4.5M |
| Currently captured (D) | $3M | $3.5M |
| **Untapped (E)** | **$8.2M** | **$1.0M** |
| Competitive position (F) | 5 | 6 |
| Capability (G) | 6 | 5 |
| Relationship (H) | 3 | 6 |
| CSAT (I) | 4 | 7 |
| **Ability to Win (J)** | **4.5** | **6.0** |
| **Quadrant** | **Pursue Aggressively** | **Maintain Share** |

Account A has more money at stake but a weaker relationship — pursue aggressively with a
structured account plan. Account B has a strong relationship but limited upside — protect the
revenue without over-investing.

## Part 4: Account Classification (Platinum → Bronze)

> **Note:** These are annual incremental revenue targets used for account planning and resource
> allocation — different from TCV thresholds used in Part 10 for RFP pursuit decisions.
> A Gold account ($5–8M incremental revenue target per year) may generate a $30M TCV deal
> over a 5-year contract — which would qualify as a Platinum RFP pursuit in Part 10.

| Tier | Annual Incremental Revenue Target | Exec Sponsor | Pursuit Approach |
|------|----------------------------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Platinum** | ≥$8M | CEO | Platinum/Gold Win Team; full account plan; quarterly exec review |
| **Gold** | $5–8M | Geo/Vertical/Horizontal Head | Platinum/Gold Win Team; structured account plan |
| **Silver** | $2–5M | One level below Geo/Vertical Head | Sales team led; BMG (Bid Management Group) managed |
| **Bronze** | $1–2M | Account Manager | Sales team led; BMG managed |

Adjust these thresholds to match your organization's deal economics. In a SaaS business a
"Platinum" account might be $500K ARR; in infrastructure services it might be $20M+.

## Part 5: Account Planning — Sweet Spot Identification

Three steps to identify the highest-value new opportunities within an existing account:

**Step 1** — Prioritize functional areas at company level
(e.g., Finance vs. Operations vs. HR vs. IT — which has the biggest budget and the most pain?)

**Step 2** — Identify sweet spots from your major service or product lines within prioritized functions
(e.g., financial planning tools within Finance, workforce analytics within HR)

**Step 3** — Prioritize specific use cases within each sweet spot using heat maps
(e.g., budgeting automation within financial planning, attrition prediction within workforce analytics)

**Output:** An attack plan with 4-quadrant prioritization per function/service line — each
bubble representing a specific opportunity sized by incremental potential.

## Part 6: Engagement Model

A structured engagement model ensures your relationship grows beyond the day-to-day delivery
team and reaches the stakeholders who make buying decisions.

### Forum Cadence

| Forum | Frequency | Participants | Purpose |
|-------|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Steering Committee** | Quarterly | Client C-suite + Your leadership | Strategic direction, escalations, joint commitments |
| **Value Discovery Workshop** | Quarterly | Client CP + Your CP/AM | Uncover hot buttons, pipeline joint roadmap |
| **BU Steering Committee** | Monthly | Division leads + Your exec team | Performance review, SoW pipeline, CSAT |
| **Working Team Meeting** | Initially weekly, then fortnightly | Project leads + Your engagement team | Project status, approvals, issue resolution |
| **CSAT Review** | Annually | Client VP + Your CP/AM | Scores by division, pain points, corrective actions |

### Value Discovery Session — Template

Run at least quarterly. Capture 4+ hot buttons per session.

| Field | What to Capture |
|-------|----------------|
| **Client Hot Button** | A specific business or operational pain point the client has raised |
| **Your Proposed Solution** | The service, product, or approach you would bring to bear |
| **Win Theme / Value Proposition** | In one sentence: why your organization is the right choice for this |
| **Discriminators** | What makes your approach uniquely better than alternatives |
| **Proof Point** | A reference client, case study, metric, or award that substantiates the claim |
| **Elaboration** | Key details behind the proof point |
| **Result / Performance Promised** | A specific, measurable outcome you can commit to |

Track every senior-level interaction on a rolling monthly basis:

## Part 7: People, Roles & Governance

### Account Manager vs. Sales — Which Model?

| Model | Description | Best for |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Integrated** (same person does both) | Deeper single relationship; simpler coordination; limited scalability | Smaller Silver/Bronze accounts |
| **Distinct** (Sales owns NN, AM owns EE/EN) | Clearer focus; higher EE/EN growth; coordination overhead | **Recommended for Platinum/Gold** |

Regardless of model: the Account Manager and Delivery Manager should operate through a single
point of contact (SPOC) with clear cross-visibility into each other's workstreams.

### RAPID Decision Framework

Use RAPID to assign clear ownership for every major account or bid decision. Ambiguous ownership
is the single most common reason deals stall internally.

| Role | What They Do | Key Rules |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **R — Recommend** | Does 80% of the analytical work; develops the recommendation with full information access | Only one R per decision |
| **A — Agree** | Can veto in extraordinary circumstances (regulatory, legal, compliance) | Assign sparingly — overuse paralyzes decisions |
| **P — Perform** | Executes once the decision is made | May be multiple Ps |
| **I — Input** | Provides specific information that could materially change the decision | Avoid over-populating; not everyone needs an I |
| **D — Decide** | Final authority; makes the call | Only one D; place at the level where business impact, information, and reaction time intersect |

### Governance Authority Matrix

Use this as a starting template — adapt role names to your organization.
**"—" = no involvement required for this activity.**

| Activity | Exec Sponsor | Geo/Vertical Sales | AM/Client Partner | Delivery | Pre-Sales |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|------------------|----------|-----------|
| Define industry sweet spots | D | — | P | I | R |
| Define client sweet spot | I | I | P/R/D | I | R |
| Develop attack plan | D | I | P/R | I | — |
| Develop account plan and vision | D | A | P/R | I | — |
| Understand client org chart | — | — | P/R | — | — |
| Develop client stakeholder map | D | I | P/R | I | — |
| Create engagement plan | A | I | P/R/D | I | — |
| Set meeting schedule and agenda | I | I | P/R/D | I | — |

### Executive Sponsor by Account Tier

| Tier | Exec Sponsor |
|------|-------------|
| Platinum | CEO |

| Silver | One level below Geo/Vertical/Horizontal Head |
| Bronze | Account Manager |

## Part 8: What Clients Look for in Strategic Partner Selection

Understanding these criteria helps you prioritize what to demonstrate at each stage of a pursuit.

| What Clients Evaluate | What to Demonstrate |
|----------------------|-------------------|
| **Client focus** | Deep understanding of their business and IT strategy; a solution tailored to their specific situation; strong client references and advocacy from similar organizations |
| **Team quality** | Executive sponsorship and visible commitment; a strong front-end engagement team; named, credible account manager and delivery leads |
| **Flexibility and pricing** | Innovative deal structure; long-term partnership orientation; willingness to share risk |
| **Capability** | Brand credibility; industry domain expertise; delivery track record; technology or methodology leadership; proven governance and transition experience; business transformation capability |

## Part 9: RFP / Bid Excellence — Four-Component Process

```
01 Opportunity Qualification → 02 Bid Planning & Management → 03 Scoping & Solutioning → 04 Bid Defense
```

### Common Failure Modes in RFP Processes

Before applying this framework, it helps to know the most common places deals are lost internally:

- Bid/no-bid decisions biased toward bidding regardless of win probability
- Client requirements synthesized superficially or not at all
- No single bid manager accountable end-to-end
- Bid teams poorly composed — too many senior people diluting focus; key specialists missing
- Win strategy developed in functional silos with no integration time
- No concise executive summary or solution highlights document
- High internal cost-per-RFP due to poor reuse of existing collateral
- Bid defense sessions not rehearsed; team unprepared for client questions

## Part 10: Deal Categorization for RFP Pursuit

> **Note:** These TCV (Total Contract Value) thresholds determine the bid pursuit team and
> governance level for a specific RFP. They are different from the annual incremental revenue
> targets in Part 4, which are used for ongoing account planning.
> Example: A Gold account (Part 4) may generate a single Platinum RFP (Part 10) if the deal
> is a multi-year managed services contract.

| Deal Tier | TCV Threshold | Criteria | Pursuit Team |
|-----------|--------------|---------|-------------|
| **Platinum** | $50M+ | Multi-service or cross-LOB (Line of Business) deals only | Platinum/Gold Win Team with Campaign Board oversight |
| **Gold** | $25–50M | Multi-service or cross-LOB deals only | Platinum/Gold Win Team |
| **Silver** | $5–25M | Any | Sales team led; BMG managed |
| **Bronze** | <$5M | Any | Sales team led; BMG managed |

> Exception: Deals involving asset or people transfer, or complex financial engineering, may be
> escalated to Platinum/Gold pursuit level regardless of TCV.

## Part 13: Component A — Opportunity Qualification

### Process Steps

**A0 — Capture Opportunity Profile**
- Scope of RFP and key information
- Deal size and expected business case (TCV and annual revenue)
- Legal constraints and submission timeframe

**A1 — Opportunity Analysis**

Complete this for each major hot button (aim for 4+). This becomes the spine of your proposal.

| Field | What to Write |
|-------|--------------|
| **Client Hot Button** | The specific business or technical pain point driving this RFP |
| **Your Proposed Solution** | The service, product, or approach you are putting forward |
| **Value Proposition** | In one sentence: the measurable benefit your solution delivers |
| **Discriminators (USP)** | What makes your solution better than the alternatives |
| **Proof Point** | A named reference, case study, certification, or measurable result |
| **Elaboration** | Key detail behind the proof point |
| **Result / Performance Achieved** | The actual metric delivered (cost saved, time reduced, uptime achieved, etc.) |

**Worked Example — Opportunity Analysis (fictional):**

| Field | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Client Hot Button | Legacy ERP causing 3-day monthly close; finance team losing 40 hours/month to manual reconciliation |
| Your Proposed Solution | Cloud ERP migration with automated reconciliation and real-time reporting |
| Value Proposition | Reduce monthly close from 3 days to same-day; eliminate 40 hours/month of manual work |
| Discriminators | Pre-built migration accelerators cut implementation time by 30% vs. competitors |
| Proof Point | Similar implementation at [Reference Client] in manufacturing sector |
| Elaboration | Migrated 8 legacy modules in 14 weeks; zero data loss; 99.8% uptime during cutover |
| Result Achieved | Monthly close reduced from 4 days to 6 hours; 98% reduction in manual reconciliation |

**A2 — Win-ability Scorecard**

Score the opportunity on two axes using the same scoring logic as Part 3:

*Opportunity Attractiveness:*
- Deal scope and client profile
- TCV and expected annual revenue
- Strategic fit with your growth priorities

*Your Ability to Win:*
- Alignment with your capability
- Strength of client relationship and access
- Intelligence on competition and your relative position
- Timeframe feasibility and legal/contractual considerations

**Governance:** Your BMG (Bid Management Group) categorizes the bid, assigns to the relevant
pursuit team, and forms the preliminary bid team. Campaign/Solution Board reviews and re-calibrates
scoring if needed. No-bid decisions escalate to Head of Large Deals or BMG lead.

## Part 15: Component C — Scoping & Solutioning

**C0 — Refine Client Understanding (Live Document)**
- Refreshed synthesis using the same eight categories from B1
- Updated after every client interaction, workshop, or advisor touchpoint
- This is a living document — not a one-time output

**C1 — Client Engagement**
- Define an engagement plan for each client workshop: objectives, your owners, clarification questions
- Capture all consolidated clarification questions:

| Category | Clarification Question | Forum | Your Owner |
|----------|----------------------|-------|-----------|
| | | | |

**C2 — Solution Development and Integration**
- Build an integrated business, technical, and operational solution
- Develop a solution roadmap covering all service lines with key client outcomes at each stage

**C3 — Reviews (Three-Stage)**

| Review | Purpose | Who Reviews |
|--------|---------|------------|
| **Blue Team** | Validate win strategy thoroughness and competitive advantage | Exec sponsor(s), LOB heads, Sales AM from same vertical in a different geography, LOB nominated expert |
| **Pink Team** | Evaluate proposal approach and solution highlights; stress-test the executive summary from the client's point of view | Exec sponsor, Sales AM, LOB nominated expert, VSG (Vertical Solutions Group) nominated expert |
| **Red Team** | Evaluate full proposal for client focus, completeness, and articulation of win strategy | Exec sponsor(s), LOB nominated expert, technology architect, VSG expert, CoE/practice experts, HR, technical writer |

### Blue Team Review Agenda (Win Strategy)
- Opportunity profile and OA scorecard review
- Client understanding: hot buttons, hygiene factors, business case
- Win strategy: themes, competitive review, positive and negative discriminators
- High-level value proposition, delivery approach, commercial structure
- Risk mitigation and key success factors

### Pink Team Review Agenda (Proposal Approach)
- Brief sales cycle history and win strategy summary
- Refined client understanding
- Proposal summary and solution highlights across all service lines
- Delivery risks, governance, transition strategy, resourcing plan
- Revenue profile, cost model, commercial risks
- Sales and client/advisor engagement plan

## Part 19: Adapting This Framework to Your Organization

This framework is industry-agnostic. Whether you sell IT services, consulting, SaaS, engineering,
or professional services — the structure applies unchanged. Here is how to make it yours in five steps.

### Step 1 — Map Your Deal Tiers to Real Numbers

Replace the illustrative thresholds in Parts 4 and 10 with your own deal economics:

| Your Tier Name | Annual Incremental Revenue Target (Part 4) | TCV Threshold (Part 10) | Your Pursuit Lead |
|---------------|------------------------------------------|------------------------|------------------|
| Strategic / Tier 1 | e.g., >$5M | e.g., >$20M | CEO or Business Unit Head |
| Major / Tier 2 | e.g., $2–5M | e.g., $8–20M | Practice Head or Regional VP |
| Standard / Tier 3 | e.g., $500K–2M | e.g., $2–8M | Sales Manager |
| Transactional | e.g., <$500K | e.g., <$2M | Account Executive |

### Step 2 — Pre-Load Your ICP Hot Buttons

Before any RFP or account planning session, document the 3–5 hot buttons your target buyers
consistently raise. These feed directly into the Opportunity Analysis (Part 13) and Win Strategy
(Part 14). Common patterns by sector:

| Sector | Typical Hot Buttons |
|--------|-------------------|
| Enterprise IT Services | Cost reduction, offshore risk, CSAT, compliance, vendor consolidation |
| Cloud / SaaS | Time-to-value, integration complexity, data security, scalability, TCO vs. build |
| Consulting / Advisory | Speed of insight, implementation risk, change management, ROI proof |
| Engineering / Manufacturing | Quality, supply chain risk, lead time, regulatory compliance |
| Healthcare | Data governance, HIPAA compliance, interoperability, patient outcomes |
| Financial Services | Regulatory risk, audit trail, data residency, operational resilience |

### Step 3 — Assign Your Governance Roles

Replace the illustrative role names in Parts 7 and 11 with your actual org structure:

- **Executive Sponsor** → Who carries the client relationship at C-level in your org?
- **Deal Principal / Bid Lead** → Who owns the RFP response end-to-end?
- **Solution Manager** → Who architects the technical or service solution?
- **Commercial Manager** → Who owns pricing, deal structure, and risk?
- **Account Manager** → Who manages the ongoing post-win relationship?

### Step 4 — Set Your Review Cadence

- **Platinum / Gold deals:** All three reviews — Blue, Pink, Red — are mandatory
- **Silver deals:** Pink + Red minimum
- **Bronze / transactional deals:** Single internal review before submission is sufficient
- **72-hour turnaround:** Standardized template only — no new reviews, reuse existing collateral

### Step 5 — Build Your Proof Point Library

The Opportunity Analysis (Part 13) and Proposal Summary (Part 14 B5) both require Proof Points.
Before your first live use of this framework, compile:

- 3–5 named client references by industry or use case (with permission to cite)
- Key outcome metrics from past wins: cost saved, cycle time reduced, ROI delivered, uptime achieved
- Relevant certifications, accreditations, or analyst recognition your buyers care about
- Any relevant awards, rankings, or independent third-party validations
